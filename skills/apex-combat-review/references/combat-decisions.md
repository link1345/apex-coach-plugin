# Combat Decisions

## Push, hold, or reset

Do not decide from a shield crack or distance alone. Compare:

- enemy damage state and reset behavior;
- squad counts, knocks, and revive state;
- player health, shield, ammo, weapons, and abilities;
- ally line of sight and time to trade;
- travel time, doors, climbs, and exposed crossings;
- enemy cover, crossfire, and retreat path;
- grenades, scans, movement tools, and team utility;
- third-party audio, ring pressure, and escape route.

A push is reasonable when resources, ally timing, enemy vulnerability, and the route support it. Holding is reasonable when current pressure wins value without exposing the team. Resetting is reasonable when survivability, support, angle control, or external risk makes commitment lower value.

## Revive windows

When allies are knocked or reviving, identify who can immediately trade. A player buying time may be more valuable than chasing a kill. Judge whether the held angle protects the revive route and whether moving wider removes the only active defender.

## Cover and peeking

Name the enemy line, cover object, exposure duration, objective, and return route. Avoid arbitrary coverage percentages or fixed numbers of corners.

A peek is safer when it isolates the required line, has a purpose, ends after the useful shot window, and preserves a return path. It is punishable when it exposes multiple lines, repeats a pre-aimed timing, or prevents allies from trading.

## Movement and high ground

High ground is valuable only when it provides pressure, information, or a safe exit. Before recommending a climb, drop, reposition, or movement ability, pass the feasibility gate. Identify the next cover and whether the player can arrive before the opportunity closes.

## Preserve partial successes

Split sequences into decisions. Retreating to cover can be correct even if the player then fails to heal. A protected door peek can be correct even if a later wide swing is not. State what to keep and what to change.
